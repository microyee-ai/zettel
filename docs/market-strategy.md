# Zettel market strategy

Research snapshot: **October 5, 2026**. Status: **proposed product and commercial strategy**. Primary-source observations are separated from recommendations below. This extends, and does not replace, the [full product brief](product-research.md). Evidence, source dates and unresolved source gaps are in [market evidence](research/2026-10-05-market-evidence.md). No market-size, adoption, revenue or product-readiness claim is established by this document.

Zettel should make the work itself durable: capture a task quickly, turn a specification into a delivery plan, work locally, and add team services when needed. The proposed position is **a work system you own, with AI that can move projects forward under your control**. The destination remains Linear-quality execution combined with programmable documents and tables, across web, desktop and customer-controlled hosting.

## Customers and the job to win

These segments are hypotheses for discovery, not measured demand or contractual scale limits.

| Segment | Job and buying trigger | First outcome to demonstrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solopreneur / independent developer | Coordinate client work and product work without losing decisions across notes, chat and tickets; prefer a durable local purchase | Turn a brief into a project, deliver its tickets, retrieve the original decisions and export everything |
| Small product team, roughly 2–20 people | Bring a founder, engineers, designer and AI assistants into one accountable delivery loop | Shared backlog, cycles, a living spec, code links and a trustworthy release update |
| Midsize organization, roughly 20–250 people | Keep multiple teams, dependencies, access boundaries and delivery health coherent; control where data is operated | Cross-team planning with restricted projects, identity lifecycle, audit and exercised recovery |

Win the solo workflow first while retaining the team data model and all full-product requirements. Keep daily capture simple, and progressively reveal project and organization controls. “Solo first” describes the adoption path; it does not delete enterprise or programmable-document scope.

## Product promise and brand direction

Proposed lead message: **Keep work yours. Move it forward.** Supporting copy: “Tickets, projects and living documents. A clear place to plan, with AI when you need it.” Product claims must reflect the client and release actually available.

Use an original visual identity built around a folded note and the letter Z: the note represents retained context, the diagonal suggests work moving through a workflow. Favor calm ink, warm paper and a distinct accent; use familiar status shapes and readable text rather than making every interaction a brand gesture. These are design proposals for the brand owner, not a trademark clearance.

The landing page should show one coherent workflow: a real ticket linked to a spec and project, a small agent proposal with a visible review step, and a downloadable app or working browser entry point. Show free versus paid boundaries clearly. A download call to action should resolve to an installable release with platform, version and integrity information. If only source or a development build exists, identify that exact artifact and its support state.

## Proposed commercial model

The following is a **pricing experiment**, not a launched offer or revenue forecast. The existing Apache-2.0 source license remains unchanged. Official distribution/support entitlement is distinct from rights to use, modify and redistribute Apache-licensed code. [License text](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0)

| Offer hypothesis | Test price, USD | Proposed value and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | Useful local work management, documents, import/export, open self-hosting and MCP; no artificial deletion or loss of local data when paid services end |
| Desktop | **$49 once**, proposed starting price; test willingness to pay before launch | Official packaged distribution and an explicitly defined update/support entitlement; retain the obtained version indefinitely. Define supported OS versions and the support period before sale. It does not revoke free source rights or include unlimited hosted services |
| Team Cloud | **$8/member/month billed annually**, test willingness to pay before launch | Managed collaboration, sync, attachments, managed backups and service operation, with explicit quotas, retention and service terms; customer-hosted capability remains an option |
| Managed AI | Usage-priced add-on; no amount chosen | Measured provider cost plus service margin, visible budget cap and usage log. Bring-your-own-provider/local inference remains an alternative where supported |
| Deployment and support | Quote per scope | Migrations, onboarding, dedicated deployments, training and contracted support; service commitments follow measured operational evidence |

The Desktop name must not conceal a donation if it is sold as a product. Define the deliverable, customer benefit, update period and refund conditions. A signed entitlement may manage access to official update/support services; its expiry must not destroy work or disable Apache-granted source rights. Do not promise perpetual support, all future operating systems, lifetime cloud hosting or unbounded AI in a one-time purchase.

Proposed pricing decision test: interview at least five solo builders and five teams using their current projects; show real free/desktop/service alternatives with their limitations; ask for a concrete choice and paid-pilot intent. Record objections and abandoned migrations. Estimate payment fees, platform signing, support labor, infrastructure, inference, taxes/refunds and security maintenance before accepting money. Competitive prices anchor the interview, not its conclusion.

## Experience and delivery contract

| Surface | Required outcome | Evidence before claiming availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web app | Create and complete real work in a browser with honest storage/sync state | Browser scenario with reload, errors, export and supported offline behavior; persistent backend required before claiming shared team work |
| Desktop app | Install, create a workspace and retain work across process/OS restart | Actual target-OS install/open test, durable data path, recovery, platform integrity/signing status and upgrade evidence |
| Localhost / self-host | Own operational control without mandatory proprietary service dependency | Clean-host installation, authentication boundary, persistent volumes, backup/restore, documented update path and network-restricted test |
| AI and MCP | Let an assistant discover work, propose/create scoped tickets and report outcomes | Real protocol-client test, permission denial, idempotent retry, actor/provenance history and disabled-AI workflow |
| Commercial services | Pay for a documented deliverable and recover the entitlement | Test checkout, authenticated webhook verification, replay safety, receipt, entitlement, refund/revocation behavior and support terms |
| Public landing on Vercel | Discover the product and reach the correct working artifact | Live deployed URL, asset/navigation/mobile checks, accurate metadata and claims, plus tested app/download destinations |

A locally persisted browser prototype, a desktop wrapper, a container recipe and a public landing page each prove different things. They do not collectively prove collaborative team delivery, recoverable storage or a sold license without the associated scenarios.

## Required scope and proposed sequencing

The [capability ledger](research/capability-ledger.md) is the implementation handoff. Every family stays in scope even when its delivery is later. It separates baseline requirements from added proposals, lists acceptance scenarios, and defaults every row to **researched / unverified implementation** until the coordinator attaches evidence.

1. **Personal project loop:** install/open, capture work, project and issue lifecycle, dependencies, a linked specification, search, export/restore, optional assistant connection and deliberate upgrade path.
2. **Small-team pilot:** shared identity/workspaces, concurrent edits, permissions, cycles, milestones, comments, intake, imports, code links, risk/change records and delivery evidence.
3. **Programmable workspace and midsize controls:** relational tables, formulas, forms/buttons, automation/connector history, portfolio/resource views, SSO/SCIM, audit/retention and operational reliability.
4. **Full parity program:** remaining advanced analytics, customer workflows, broader integrations, mobile/desktop polish, release/coding-agent workflows and independent family-by-family validation.

The sequence is a planning proposal; it does not postpone recording the full ambition or authorize a false “complete” label after the first phase. Architecture choices belong in [decisions](decisions/), and each implementation issue needs a user outcome, dependency list, scenario and evidence.
