# Zettel desktop launch playbook

Prepared **7 October 2026**. Status: proposed operating plan. No interviews, outreach, promotion, payments or launch experiments have been run under this plan. Owners below are role assignments for coordination, not claims that people have been hired. Start the 90-day clock when the pilot entry gate passes; if it does not, move the launch date.

The [dated desktop research](research/2026-10-07-desktop-market.md) contains current sources, corrected Anttable/Graphite comparables, price hypotheses and cost sensitivities. The [product brief](product-research.md) retains the full Linear plus programmable-document ambition. The [brand guide](brand.md) controls the original visual identity. This plan gives that ambition a specific initial customer and a measurable adoption path.

## Audience, job and position

**Initial customer hypothesis:** an independent software developer or technical founder who owns one to three active products/client projects, works mainly on a laptop, already maintains tasks plus written decisions, and can evaluate a desktop preview without a team migration. The trigger is a real release or client delivery in the next month, not a general desire to “get organized.” Start with the OS/architecture actually verified in the release record; record unsupported-platform demand rather than implying support.

Job statement: **“When I pick a project back up, help me see what to do next and why, then keep my plan and decisions with the work I complete.”** The alternatives to investigate are an existing issue tracker plus notes, a personal task app, Markdown/checklists, and doing nothing. Ask users to show the last time this failed instead of assuming dissatisfaction with their existing tool.

Proposed category: **a local project workspace for independent builders**. Position: **“Plan the work. Keep the context.”** Zettel brings tickets, projects and linked notes into the same working session, with local data and optional assistant access. The advantage to prove is quicker, more reliable resumption of a real project, not a generic promise to be “AI-native” or unique because it works offline.

For this launch, a “team” is a discovery cohort, not a supported collaboration claim. Track demand from small teams and enterprise operators in separate discovery notes. Their needed permissions, identity, shared data, programmable documents, automation and controlled deployment remain product requirements; the desktop launch is not completion of that scope.

## Brand and message system

Keep the folded-ticket Z, existing typography, dark neutral surfaces and restrained accent. The visual story is a working instrument: readable issue rows, a selected decision note, and a clear next action. Show the actual app at readable size. Use the user's own project language in examples; mark invented sample records as examples. Do not borrow Anttable's comparison-table promise or Graphite's self-organizing notebook identity.

| Message layer | Proposed copy | Proof / boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page title / search description | “Zettel — local project workspace for independent builders” | “Local” refers to the selected client/storage mode; release notes explain browser/desktop differences. |
| Hero | **“Plan the work. Keep the context.”** | Customer-message experiment against the existing “A sharper way to move work forward.” Neither is a measured winner. |
| Supporting line | “Tickets, projects and linked notes for your next release. Work on your computer, with optional AI when you choose it.” | Show all three in one real workflow. Explain provider setup and outgoing AI requests near that feature. |
| Primary CTA | “Try the desktop preview” | Only when it resolves to the verified artifact/version/OS. Otherwise use “Open the browser workspace” and label its storage. |
| Secondary CTA | “See a project in Zettel” | Opens a working sample clearly separate from the user's records. No email gate for the demo. |
| Trust detail | “Single-user preview. No automatic sync or team collaboration yet. Export backups from Settings.” | Keep this close to the CTA and download information, not hidden in a footer. |
| Pricing during discovery | “The preview is free. We’re evaluating paid desktop support and optional services.” | The $49 concept is a research offer, not a purchase button. If retained publicly, label it “Proposed — not for sale.” |
| Roadmap | “Working toward shared projects and programmable documents.” | Link the capability ledger and distinguish plans from current notes. No dates without delivery evidence. |

Voice: short, concrete, composed. Name the action and what happened. “Ticket saved locally” is useful only when the active storage mode supports that statement. “AI proposed four tickets; review before saving” applies to the proposal flow; direct MCP writes need their own explicit tool-permission explanation. Avoid “everything stays on your device” when an optional provider can receive context, and avoid “encrypted,” “secure,” “enterprise-ready,” “native,” “instant” or performance claims without the specific evidence.

Do not create testimonial placeholders, invented logos, user counters, discounts with artificial deadlines, countdowns or unverified comparisons. Use the working product, source availability, documented recovery and clear limitations as early proof. “Zettel” has not received naming/trademark clearance; descriptive metadata improves discoverability but does not settle name rights.

## Launch entry gate and demo

The release manager owns an evidence packet for the exact downloadable build. The verifier independently checks installation, first launch, persistence, export/restore and upgrade on each advertised platform. The security reviewer checks artifact integrity, signature status, dependency notices and the local/assistant boundary. Preview distribution may accurately disclose unsigned/ad-hoc limitations; a commercial trusted-installer claim needs its own signing/notarization evidence. [Release record](release-preview.md), [paid-launch gates](paid-launch.md).

Use this two-minute demo script, with real captures from the verified build:

1. Start a new workspace and create a project named “Ship a client portal.”
2. Write a short note recording the agreed scope; add three tickets, linking relevant context. Show the supported linkage, not a future programmable table mockup.
3. Set a priority/dependency, select the next ticket and move it to completion.
4. Quit and reopen the app; show the same work. Export a backup and point to the restore guide.
5. If separately verified, show an optional AI proposal and review; explain who provides/pays for the model. Keep the core demo useful without a key.

Give testers one written task, then observe without coaching: “Use Zettel to plan the next piece of a real project, close it, come back, and find the decision behind that task.” Record where they stop. A demo of example data is not counted as activation.

## Measurement contract

**North-star proposal:** weekly builders advancing a real project. Count a consenting pilot participant once in a seven-day period if they return on a different day to a self-created project and change or complete at least one ticket connected to their work. Sample workspaces, automated tests, the maintainer and repeated events from one session do not count. This definition measures use, not proof that Zettel caused a shipped release.

| Measure | Definition | Evidence and caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Install success | Completed first open / attempted installs, by version and OS | Tester report or observed session. A downloaded file is not an install. |
| Activation | Within seven days: self-created project + three relevant tickets + one linked note + at least one ticket progressed | Consent-based session/diary; do not count seeded data. |
| D7 retention | Activated people returning to advance real work during days 7–13 after activation / activated people whose entire window has elapsed | Settled cohorts only. Always report counts, not a percentage alone. |
| D30 retention | Equivalent activity during days 28–34 after activation | The final cohort must mature before a day-90 decision. |
| Context recovery | Participant finds the decision behind their chosen ticket without help | Observe success, time and confusion; the target is a diagnostic, not a published speed claim. |
| Support burden | Staff minutes for install, usage, recovery and commercial help / cohort participant or purchaser | Include founder labor; separate unpaid research interview time. |
| Qualified commercial intent | Retained user chooses a stated offer and accepts a follow-up about that offer, after explaining its terms | An interview answer or CTA click is not a purchase. Record “neither.” |
| Acquisition efficiency | Staff hours and cash per activated participant, then per retained participant, by channel | Referral self-report is acceptable; last-click attribution is not causality. |

No analytics implementation is implied. Begin with voluntary interviews and weekly short diaries stored in a private, access-controlled research location; keep only anonymized aggregate findings in the repository. Ask permission before recording or viewing confidential work. A future telemetry proposal must minimize fields to event/version/platform/coarse timing with informed opt-in, easy opt-out, a defined retention period and no ticket/note content, paths, prompts, provider keys or fingerprinting. Local work must remain usable without participation.

## Discovery interview protocol

Proposed first cohort: **12 independent builders**, split across issue-tracker-plus-notes, personal-task-plus-notes, and Markdown/manual workflows (four each). Include at least four people who already pay for a comparable tool; do not recruit only friends or enthusiastic AI users. Add **four small-team leads** in a separate future-collaboration discovery track; do not combine their answers with solo pricing. These are recruitment targets, not a claimed representative sample.

Thirty-five-minute session:

1. **Consent and fit, 3 minutes.** Confirm current project, role, supported computer and permission to observe. Permit a sanitized project or verbal account; do not require private source code or client details.
2. **Last actual incident, 8 minutes.** “Show me the last time you returned to a project and had to reconstruct what to do.” Where was the decision? What happened, how often, what workaround, and what concrete consequence? Ask what works well today.
3. **Current economics, 4 minutes.** What do they personally pay? Which tool would be displaced? What prevents switching? Record amount and billing basis as their report, not audited spend.
4. **Uncoached task, 10 minutes.** Run the entry-gate task on a real/sanitized project. Note exact steps, recovery, confusion and whether linked context matters. Avoid describing future collaboration as available.
5. **Offer comparison, 7 minutes.** Show both cards in randomized order: (A) $49 once, 12-month official updates/support, $29 optional manual renewal, keep acquired versions; (B) free official local desktop, possible sync/backup $5/month or $48/year when built. Show free source builds and “neither/keep my current tools” on both. Ask what they expect at expiry, whether they need sync at all, what would justify payment, and their preferred next step. No card, deposit, pre-order or fake checkout.
6. **Close, 3 minutes.** Ask what would make them stop using it and whether they choose to try their project for two weeks. A follow-up occurs only with separate consent and communication authorization.

Use a consistent record: anonymous participant ID; segment/current alternative; incident and consequence; task outcome; exact objections; card order; choice/terms comprehension; follow-up consent; researcher inference clearly separated from quotes. Do not enter raw personal details in Git. Incentives, if later approved, compensate time equally regardless of response.

Twelve interviews can reject a weak story and surface failure modes; they cannot prove market fit or a precise price optimum. Later compare $29/$49/$79 purchase cards or alternative service prices with new participants to avoid anchoring the original group. Change one variable at a time, preserve “neither,” and report counts and uncertainty. Do not label a small qualitative exercise a statistically significant A/B test.

## Channels and concrete experiments

Use two channels initially. The proposed channel mix follows the desktop builder audience, not Anttable's consumer comparison communities or Graphite's learning audience. All goals and time budgets below are judgments to test, not industry benchmarks or spending authorization.

| Priority | Experiment / artifact | Outcome to measure | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One runnable builder demo + install/recovery guide linked from the repository; one founder-written account of a real project workflow | Ten external install attempts; activation and D7 retention | Fix failed installs or unclear storage before increasing reach. GitHub stars do not replace retained use. |
| 2 | Two helpful developer-community posts, where current rules permit and authorship is disclosed; demonstrate resuming a project with its decisions | Ten qualified pilot volunteers over four weeks; staff hours per activated participant | If fewer than three activate after ten completed attempts, inspect fit/onboarding rather than posting more. No unsolicited private messages. |
| 3 | One Show HN of the runnable app after entry gate; founder available to discuss it | Completed installs, real-project activations and next-week returns | No landing-page-only submission or vote solicitation. HN asks for a usable artifact and low trial barriers. [Official Show HN rules, checked Oct 7](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html) |
| 4 | Two task-focused guides: “Plan a small release locally” and “Keep decisions connected to your tickets”; one dated comparison showing fit and gaps | Qualified visits that lead to retained pilot use over 30 days | Continue if at least five activate and two return; otherwise revise topic or stop. Never infer absent competitor features from a marketing omission. |
| 5 | After retention gate and coordinator assignment: prepare five individualized creator/reviewer briefs with a verified build, known limits and a two-minute demo | Review invitations accepted and attributable activated/retained users | Sending requires separate authorization. No guaranteed coverage, paid review or endorsement assumed. |

Product Hunt and broader newsletter distribution are optional day-60+ experiments only after retention, installation and support are credible. Recheck each platform's current submission rules before posting. No paid acquisition is proposed in the first 90 days: the current contribution and retention are unknown. This is an economic sequencing choice, not a claim that paid channels never work.

## The first 30, 60 and 90 days

Day 1 starts after the pilot entry gate, not automatically on October 7. Run a 30-minute weekly review: settled cohort counts, platform failures, top three blockers, support time, cost assumptions, channel effort and what was disproved. Publish only anonymized evidence in the decision log. Keep a clear distinction between “not yet tested,” “failed,” and “passed.”

| Window | Work and proposed capacity | Evidence target / threshold | Decision at the end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–30: establish a real use loop | 6 researcher hours/week, plus engineering work assigned through triage. Run 12 solo interviews and four team interviews; observe at least ten installs across supported targets; make demo/recovery guide; test the two hero messages for comprehension, five users each. | At least 9/10 observed installs complete; at least 8/12 solo participants complete the core task; at least 8/10 message-test participants accurately state audience, local storage and preview limitations. Zero unresolved data loss. Counts are directional. | If install/task threshold fails, fix and repeat the affected scenario. If fewer than 4/12 report a concrete repeated context-loss problem, revise the segment/job before scaling. |
| Days 31–60: test return and offer | 4 researcher + 4 distribution hours/week. Enroll 30 solo pilot participants across at least two acquisition sources. Collect settled D7 cohorts, support minutes and price-card choices from at least 15 retained participants as they become available. Publish only approved demo/community material. | At least 20 activations and D7 retention of 40% or more across at least 20 matured activated participants (8/20 minimum); no unresolved data-loss incident; declining install support; record both card choice and comprehension. | Below 25% D7 retention after 20 matured activations: stop expansion and diagnose product/fit. Between 25–39%: continue limited testing. Small samples remain uncertain; do not declare market fit. |
| Days 61–90: choose commercial direction | 4 researcher + 4 distribution hours/week, with capacity reduced if support exceeds budget. Seek 60 total pilot participants, improve import/recovery friction, and mature 30 activated participants to D30. Re-run revised offers on new participants. | D30 retention at least 30% over at least 30 matured activations (9/30 minimum); 20 retained-user offer interviews; concrete support/service-cost estimates; five willing future paid-pilot participants for the preferred offer, with terms understood. | Prefer free desktop/services if retained users repeatedly choose a specific service and conservative unit economics work. Advance $49 only if at least 10/20 retained interviewees choose it over service/neither and at least five accept a future pilot discussion with correct expiry expectations. Neither threshold is purchase validation. |

If cohort maturity takes longer, the review moves beyond day 90. Do not fill missing rows with assumed retention. If no offer earns a clear, comprehended choice, keep the preview free and revisit value/segment; do not add an artificial export limit. Paid demand is only tested when a later authorized, operationally ready paid pilot has actual purchases, refunds and retention. The paid-launch checklist remains in force for that future work.

## Budget and decision record

The plan allocates roughly **88 researcher/distribution hours** over three four-week phases at the capacities above: `6×4 + 8×4 + 8×4`. Weekly reviews are included in those hours; engineering and support are additional. At the research model's $60/hour assumption that is **$5,280 of opportunity cost**, even with no ads. The remaining calendar days allow cohort follow-up rather than assuming a 90-day window is exactly 12 weeks. Proposed optional interview incentives: up to 16 × $25 = **$400**, if separately approved; do not incur this from the plan itself. Signing, hosting, domain, merchant, support and release costs need actual quotes/decisions; none were purchased here.

Before selecting a channel expansion, check that its audience fits the supported app and that the founder can handle the support load. The first launch succeeds by creating a repeatable useful desktop workflow and trustworthy evidence; it does not by itself verify the complete work OS or prove market fit.
